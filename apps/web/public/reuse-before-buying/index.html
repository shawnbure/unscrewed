Reuse field note · September 21, 2026

Before you buy, run a five-minute reuse check.

Buying can be the right answer. It just should not be the automatic answer when the useful thing—or the skill to restore it—may already be nearby.

The check is intentionally small. It is not a pledge to stop buying or a claim that every object can be saved. It is a way to notice options before a rushed purchase creates another duplicate, another repairable discard, or another item used once and stored for years.

1. Check the shelf.

Do you already own something that solves most of the problem? Search the garage, closet, tool bag, spare-parts bin, or office before shopping. The forgotten version is often good enough for the job.

2. Check the repair.

Name the actual failure: a cable, seal, fastener, battery, switch, zipper, wheel, or missing instruction. If the repair is safe and proportionate, the needed part or skill may be much smaller than the replacement.

3. Check the neighbor.

For a short job, borrowing can be more sensible than owning. Ask for the exact item, duration, pickup boundary, and condition in which you will return it. Specific requests are easier to answer than “Does anyone have tools?”

4. Check the trade.

Offer something concrete in return: another useful item, an hour of a named skill, delivery, setup, or a repair you can actually perform. State condition, location, timing, and the outcome you will accept so another person can make a real proposal.

5. Check the handoff.

Agree on the exchange before meeting. Use a public place when appropriate, keep personal information limited, inspect items, and leave if the terms change or the situation feels wrong. A good reuse decision still needs a safe, two-sided handoff.

If none of those routes works, buy what you need without guilt. The point is not purity. The point is giving useful goods and practical skills one fair chance to stay in circulation.
