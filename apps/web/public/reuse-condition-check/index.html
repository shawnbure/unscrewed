Reuse field note · October 2, 2026

Describe condition before you offer a used item.

“Used” is not a condition. A neighbor should be able to tell what works, what does not, what was tested, and what they may need to repair before making a proposal.

A clear condition note is useful whether the item is free, borrowed, swapped, or traded for a skill. It reduces surprise at the handoff and gives repairable things a better chance of reaching someone who actually wants the work.

Photograph the wear.

Show the whole item first, then the scratches, cracks, missing pieces, labels, plugs, connectors, moving parts, and any area that may change the other person's decision. Do not hide damage with a flattering angle.

Name the fault in plain language.

Say “the switch turns on but the blade does not move,” not “probably an easy fix.” If you do not know the cause, say so. A diagnosis is evidence only when someone has actually tested it.

State what you tested—and what you did not.

Record the last successful use, the power or battery check, the parts included, and the limits of your inspection. “Untested because the charger is missing” is more useful than “as is.”

Separate cosmetic wear from a safety concern.

A faded surface is different from exposed wiring, a damaged pressure vessel, a compromised helmet, or a recalled product. Do not offer something whose safe condition you cannot reasonably establish.

Make the requested return just as concrete.

Name the item, skill, time, or help you would accept. Include location, timing, pickup expectations, and the result that would complete the exchange. The other person needs enough information to answer without guessing.

Good condition notes do not guarantee a match. They do make an honest no easier and a serious proposal more likely. That is a better starting point than treating every object as either perfect or trash.

Give one useful thing a fair description.

If you are in Chandler or the East Valley, publish one genuine local offer with condition, location, timing, and a specific requested return. If the exchange is remote, make sure the deliverable and completion test can truly happen remotely.
