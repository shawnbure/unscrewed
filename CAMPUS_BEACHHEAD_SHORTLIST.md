# Beachhead Campus — Ranked Shortlist

Scored on the five things that make a barter marketplace ignite: **walkable density**, **active online community** (proof of latent demand), **an institutional partner already primed** (sustainability/move-out reuse), **aligned culture**, and **a self-contained market** (isolated college town beats commuter school).

---

## #1 — University of Massachusetts Amherst  ★ recommended

Everything you'd want in a first beachhead lines up here.

- **Massive, predictable trade event.** ~13,000 students move out every spring — a wall of lightly-used furniture, fridges, and textbooks. That churn *is* your launch. "Don't dump it — trade it" writes itself.
- **The institutional partner already exists and is award-winning.** UMass's student-run **New2U** program (Office of Waste Management + Sustainability + student interns) diverted 100,000+ lbs from landfills and won the 2025 Campus Race to Zero Waste case-study title. They are actively looking for ways to reduce move-out waste — you're free matchmaking infrastructure that furthers their mission. This is a warm partner, not a cold pitch.
- **Proven latent demand.** Active **r/umass** subreddit *and* an existing **"UMass Free And For Sale"** Facebook group with thousands of members — people are already trading; you just give them a better home.
- **Walkable, self-contained density.** Amherst is a college town, and UMass's Southwest is one of the largest residential complexes in the country. Thousands of traders inside a 15-minute walk.
- **Five-college bonus.** Amherst, Hampshire, Smith, Mount Holyoke, and Amherst College share the valley and a free bus — a natural, contiguous expansion path once UMass is won.
- **Culture fit.** Progressive, sustainability-minded, mutual-aid-friendly. The manifesto resonates.

**First three contacts here:** the New2U / Office of Sustainability staff lead; Residence Life (Southwest first); the campus paper (Daily Collegian / Amherst Bulletin, which already covers New2U).

---

## #2 — University of Vermont (Burlington)
Strong culture fit — Burlington is arguably the most barter-friendly college town in America (deep mutual-aid, anti-corporate, environmental roots). Mid-size (~11k), walkable, active subreddit. Slightly smaller institutional move-out infrastructure than UMass, but the *cultural* soil is the richest on this list. A close second, and the best pick if you have any personal tie to Vermont.

## #3 — Western Washington University (Bellingham)
PNW sustainability model, ~15k students, aligned counterculture, isolated walkable town. Excellent culture and density; farther from you logistically if you're East Coast, and its trade community is less proven online than UMass's.

## #4 — CU Boulder
Huge sustainability culture and a very active subreddit, but larger (~30k+) and more sprawling — harder for a solo founder to *dominate* as a first beachhead. Great candidate #2 or #3 in the expansion phase.

---

## Deliberately not #1: UT Austin / Northeastern / big urban schools
UT Austin has great reuse infrastructure (Trash to Treasure, $1 resale) and a massive audience — but at 40k+ students in a huge city, you can't saturate it on <$500 and part-time hours. It violates the concentration rule. It's a phase-2+ target, not a first beachhead. Same logic for large urban/commuter campuses: no walkable density, no self-contained market.

---

## Recommendation

**Launch on UMass Amherst.** It's the rare campus where density, a proven trading community, *and* an award-winning institutional partner that already wants what you're offering all exist at once — which shortens your time-to-liquidity more than anything else on this list. Choose UVM instead only if you have a personal connection to Vermont (an insider is worth more than any ranking).

**Timing:** the ideal launch window is the **spring move-out spike** (with a warm-up starting ~3 weeks before) or **fall move-in**. Whichever comes first, that's your countdown.
